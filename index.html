🩸 Peripheral Blood Morphology

Real/reference smear images with concise morphology notes. Always correlate with your laboratory SOP and a qualified morphologist.

What to identify on a peripheral smear

RBC

Size, shape, colour, central pallor, inclusions and distribution.

WBC

Nuclear segmentation/shape, chromatin, cytoplasm and granules.

Platelets

Number, size, granularity and clumping.

Smear quality

Look in the appropriate monolayer area; thick areas and the feathered edge can distort morphology.

Core practical formulas

  • MCV (fL) = Hct (%) × 10 / RBC (million/µL)
  • MCH (pg) = Hb (g/dL) × 10 / RBC (million/µL)
  • MCHC (g/dL) = Hb (g/dL) × 100 / Hct (%)
  • Absolute count = Total WBC × differential % / 100
  • Corrected reticulocyte % = Retic % × patient Hct / normal Hct

Peripheral smear essentials

For routine morphology, examine a well-made, appropriately stained smear in the area where RBCs are mostly singly distributed and show appropriate central pallor. Oil-immersion microscopy is used for detailed morphology. Slide artifacts can mimic disease.

Normal mature RBCs are biconcave discs, about 7.5 µm in diameter, with central pallor about 30–45% of the cell diameter. Most platelets are about 1–3 µm.

ICSH recommends standardized nomenclature and grading for RBC, WBC and platelet morphological abnormalities.
